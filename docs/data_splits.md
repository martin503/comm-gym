# Data and splits

Card on the source dataset, the household/time/category split design, and how the tasks
consume it. Numbers from `reports/data_checks.md` (regenerated by `scripts/check_data.py`)
and `reports/splits_summary.md` (`scripts/build_splits.py`); design in
`configs/splits.yaml`, implemented in `src/commgym/dunnhumby/splits.py`.

## Source dataset

Dunnhumby **Complete Journey** — a real grocery purchase panel: 2,500 households shopping
across 582 stores over days 1–711 (weeks 1–102), 276,484 baskets, ~2.6M transaction lines,
with campaign/coupon tables and household demographics.

Cleaning (`scripts/check_data.py`): 98.16% of lines kept (2,548,090). Drops: nonpositive
quantity 14,466; nonpositive sales value 4,451; excluded departments 22,429; excluded
commodity 2,288; quantity outliers 4,008. Price derivation is validated, not assumed:
regular ≥ loyalty price on 99.9999% of lines; median unit price $2.25 regular / $1.99 with
loyalty; 50.2% of lines carry a loyalty discount; median coupon amount $1.00 (q05 $0.30,
q95 $3.00).

The **campaign window** is weeks 32–102 (day 224 onward) — the range where coupon tables
have live campaigns. 71 of the window weeks have at least one live campaign.

## Three held-out axes

Every task instance must be evaluable without leakage along three independent axes:

1. **Households** — cohort split (below): eval households never appear in training data.
2. **Time** — week cut: eval instances are drawn from a later window than training data.
3. **Categories** — held-out commodities (below): 20% of eligible categories never appear
   in any task instance, in any split.

## Cohorts and the time cut

- Households are split **80/20 into cohorts A (1,999) and B (501)**, stratified by
  early-window spend quartile × demographics-table membership, seed 42
  (`configs/splits.yaml` → `_split_cohorts` in `src/commgym/dunnhumby/splits.py`).
- The time cut is **week 68** — the midpoint of the campaign window (37 weeks before, 34
  after), chosen before any task existed in the data checks.
- The cut does not skew marginal distributions (split-balance check):

| | early (w32–68) | late (w69–102) |
|---|---|---|
| baskets | 99,976 | 90,309 |
| transaction lines | 1,019,566 | 939,794 |
| median basket spend | $15.98 | $16.55 |
| private-brand line share | 28.2% | 28.4% |
| distinct commodities | 299 | 300 |

One disclosed asymmetry: the late window is **campaign-richer** — median 157 eligible
commodities with live coupons per week vs 90 early. Eval instances therefore draw from a
richer coupon environment than training instances. This is inherent to the data (campaigns
ramp up), not a bug; it is disclosed rather than controlled.

## The four splits

Cohorts crossed with the time cut give four splits (asserted disjoint: A ∩ B = 0
households):

| split | households | weeks | baskets | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 1,999 (A) | 32–68 | 81,604 | RL training instances (only refund has them so far) |
| dev | 501 (B) | 32–68 | 18,372 | dry runs, protocol shakedown, feasibility checks |
| eval_unseen | 501 (B) | 69–102 | 17,668 | headline eval — unseen households **and** later window |
| eval_known | 1,999 (A) | 69–102 | 72,641 | control: same households as train, later window |

- **eval_unseen** holds out both novelty axes at once — no household overlap with train and
  a strictly later time window. It is the split every headline number in the task cards is
  computed on.
- **eval_known** exists as a control: same households as train, later window. Comparing
  eval_unseen against eval_known would separate household novelty from time shift. No task
  has generated eval_known instances yet; the generators support it.
- **dev** shares the time window with train but uses unseen households — cheap, leak-free
  iterations before spending eval budget.

## Held-out commodities

- **52 of 261 eligible commodities (20%, seeded draw, seed 42)** — eligible meaning ≥ 4
  priced SKUs in ≥ 10 weeks. Examples: BAKING, BATTERIES, BEEF, CAT FOOD, CHIPS&SNACKS.
- They are **never sampled into any task instance, in any split** — not as cartopt
  categories, not as offer coupon coverage, not as refund basket content. This is the
  unseen-category generalization axis, and unlike the splits it cuts across *all* splits:
  even training instances never see these categories.
- The hold-out is material: 53.96% of baskets contain at least one held-out commodity, so
  excluding them shapes the data every task sees. The same seeded rule backs a data check
  (`select_heldout_commodities`).

## How the tasks consume the splits

| task | dev | train | eval_unseen | anchoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cartopt | 30 per level × L1–L4 | — | 100 per level × L1–L4 | menus/prices anchored on real household baskets on campaign-live days |
| offer | 10 | — | 50 (evaluated at L1/L2/L3) | anchors on real campaign-live days; 150 stored references |
| refund (v4) | 100 per level × L1–L3 | 100 per level × L1–L3 | 100 per level × L1–L3 | real basket lines; evaluated at L2 only — task closed as saturated |

(cartopt and offer have no train instances yet; their generators support `--split train`,
which draws from cohort A, weeks 32–68.)

# Grounded offer messages (offer)

Task card. Numbers from the eval_unseen sweep + judging of 2026-10-24/25; the live version
is `reports/offer/offer_eval_report.md` (regenerated by `scripts/offer/analyze.py`).

## The problem it solves

A grocery retailer has this week's coupon campaigns and wants to push a short, personalized
message to each loyalty-app household. The failure modes of naive personalization are well
known: generic blasts nobody opens, and confident claims that are wrong for that household
(prices that don't apply, deadlines that don't exist, categories the customer never buys).
The task makes both failure modes explicit and gradeable.

The model plays the push-notification writer: given a household's purchase profile (top
commodities with purchase counts and store-brand shares, trips per week, 98-day history) and
a table of live coupons (code, covered categories, brand tier, ends-soon flag), it selects
the 1–3 coupons to promote and writes one message of at most 40 words. Grounding rules, all
stated in the system prompt: no digits or amounts; mention only categories covered by the
selected coupons; deadline language only if a selected coupon is flagged; brand-tier claims
must match the selection; never invent brand or product names. The reply ends with:

```text
<answer>{"selected": ["k03", "k01"], "message": "..."}</answer>
```

**Use case:** CRM push / loyalty-app notification writing, with a verifiable grounding
contract instead of vibes-based copy evaluation.

Levels are nested prefixes of one coupon pool — the most relevant coupons first, distractors
growing: **L1 = 5 coupons shown, L2 = 10, L3 = 20**. The answer must pick from exactly the
shown slice; pool order is seeded-shuffled so codes carry no positional hint.

## Reward function

```text
gate or parse failure   →  0.0                    (never sent to the judge)
otherwise               →  clamp(0.2 + 0.2·affinity + 0.6·judge_win, 0, 1)
```

- **affinity** — share of the shown pool's purchase mass (profile purchase counts) captured
  by the selected coupons. A gate-passed rollout therefore banks a prefix of 0.2–0.4 before
  any judging.
- **judge_win** — a pairwise comparison of the candidate (selection + message) against a
  stored reference for the same (instance, level), by `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` at
  temperature 0. The judge sees the household's own prompt verbatim plus both responses and
  answers A / B / TIE. Each pair is judged in **both orders** (position-bias control) and
  `judge_win` is the mean of the candidate-perspective scores (A = 1, B = 0, TIE = 0.5).
  Criteria: relevance to household habits, faithfulness to profile and coupons, clarity and
  natural tone, persuasive without pressure or flattery.
- **References** — one per (instance, level): haiku-5.5 sampled at temperature 0.7, up to 3
  attempts, first gate-clean completion kept; 8 of 150 needed a gate-clean template fallback.
- **Gate** — deterministic and lexical; any failure zeroes the rollout:

| gate failure | blocks |
|---|---|
| `bad_code:<code>` | a selected code not in the shown slice |
| `selection_count:<n>` | fewer than 1 or more than 3 selected |
| `length:<words>` | message over 40 words |
| `numbers:digit_or_symbol` / `numbers:<phrase>` | any digit, `$`, `%`, or spelled amount ("free", "half off", "percent", …); product idioms like "gluten free" are allowlisted |
| `ungrounded_category:<keyword>` | a category word whose commodities are not covered by the selected coupons (lexicon of commodity names, tokens, singulars, synonyms like soda/pop → soft drinks, chips/crisps → bag snacks) |
| `not_specific` | no category mention intersects the selection at all |
| `urgency:<phrase>` | deadline language ("ends soon", "last chance", "hurry") with no ends-soon coupon selected |
| `brand_tier:<claim>` | "store/national brand" claim not matched by a selected coupon of that tier that also covers a mentioned category |
| `judge_addressing:<phrase>` | meta-talk aimed at the grader ("evaluator", "high score", "ignore previous") |

## Why it won't be reward hacked

- **The big money is behind the gate.** Without a gate pass the reward is 0.0 — no prefix,
  no judge. The gate is deterministic lexical code (`src/commgym/offer/reward.py`,
  `lexicon.py`), not an LLM, so it cannot be sweet-talked.
- **Flattery and prompt injection are named crimes.** `judge_addressing` fails any message
  addressing the evaluator, claiming a score, or saying "ignore previous" — even though the
  judge only ever sees gate-passed messages, the gate pre-empts the attempt
  (`tests/offer/test_reward_gate.py`).
- **Numbers are banned outright** — digits and `$`/`%` characters, plus spelled amounts.
  A message cannot promise savings magnitudes it can't verify.
- **Category claims must be covered.** Every commodity-name token (and singular/synonym) is
  a keyword; each mention must intersect the selected coupons' coverage. Keyword-stuffing
  the profile's top categories while selecting cheap coupons fails the gate.
- **The judge was attacked before it was trusted.** `scripts/offer/judge_check.py` ran an
  adversarial suite against it (`reports/offer/offer_judge_reliability.md`, 120 pairs, 0
  unparseable): flattery, keyword stuffing, padded-to-40-words, emoji spam, injection
  paraphrase, wrong personalization — **every variant mean win 0.00**; the reference against
  itself scores 0.50; order consistency 100%; self-agreement 100%. Both-order judging
  controls position bias by construction.
- **Baselines bracket the scale.** A generic blast fails the gate on 100% of instances
  (final 0.00); a random gate-clean template lands at 0.22–0.24 final; the best
  affinity-greedy template at 0.29–0.32. Everything above that is earned from the judge.

**Disclosed caveats.** (1) The references are written by the same haiku model that judges —
a structural self-preference bias; win rates measure "beats haiku-style copy", and the
`reference_self` check (0.50) bounds overt self-favoritism. A cross-model agreement
spot-check (`agreement_model`, 10% sample) is configured in `judge.yaml` but not yet run.
(2) The gate is deliberately strict: any single-token category word counts as a mention, so
everyday words that happen to be commodity tokens ("new" → New Age beverages) can trip
`ungrounded_category`. That strictness is pre-registered, not tuned post-hoc — see failure
example 1.

## Instances

| split | instances | note |
|---|---|---|
| dev | 10 | cohort B households, weeks 32–68 |
| eval_unseen | 50, each evaluated at all of L1/L2/L3 | cohort B households, weeks 69+ (unseen households, later window); anchors on real campaign-live days |
| references | 150 | one gate-clean reference per instance × level |
| train | 0 generated so far | generator supports `--split train` (cohort A, weeks 32–68) |

20% of eligible commodities are held out (seeded) and never sampled — an unseen-category
generalization axis.

```json

```

**Why final reward 1.0 = 0.2 + 0.2·affinity + 0.6·judge_win with affinity = 1.0 and
judge_win = 1.0:**

- **Affinity 1.0**: the pool's purchase mass for this household sits in Oral Hygiene (5

  it. Prefix = 0.2 + 0.2 = 0.4.

  vegetables" → k01's Organics Fruit Vegetables. "these deals end soon" is legal urgency:
  every selected coupon carries the ends-soon flag. 13 words, no digits.
- **Judge_win 1.0**: beat the stored reference in both presentation orders.
- Final = 0.2 + 0.2·1.0 + 0.6·1.0 = **1.0**.

**Why reward 0.0** — the gate zeroes everything; a would-be prefix of
0.2 + 0.2·0.648 = 0.33 (the affinity was decent) is never awarded, and the rollout is never

- "snacks" **fails**: it lexically denotes BAG SNACKS-type commodities — and the selected

  fine; the wording wasn't: "snack nuts" would have passed.
- "new deals" **fails**: "new" is a token of the commodity "New Age" (beverages), so it

This is qwen's single dominant failure mode (nearly all of its gate losses look like this) —
one learnable rule: mention exactly what the coupons cover.

### Failure 3 — gate-clean but judged worse than the reference, `openai/gpt-6-luna`

Same household as failure 1 (`offer-eval_unseen-710-615`), rollout 1/8, L2 — showing the
layer above the gate. Prompt: see failure 1. Selected k03 + k01 (soft drinks, frozen
pizza):

```json
<answer>{"selected":["k03","k01"],"message":"Find offers on soft drinks and frozen pizza in your coupons."}</answer>
```

**Why final reward 0.33 = 0.2 + 0.2·0.648 + 0.6·0.0:**

- **Gate clean**: "soft drinks" → k03, "frozen pizza" → k01; 11 words, no digits, no
  urgency. The base 0.2 is banked.
- **Affinity 0.648**: the two selected coupons capture 65% of the shown pool's purchase
  mass → prefix = 0.2 + 0.1296 = 0.33.
- **Judge_win 0.0**: lost the pairwise comparison in *both* orders against the stored
  reference:

> Stocking up on soft drinks or frozen pizza this week? Fresh savings are waiting for your
> favorite sodas and frozen pizzas. Check your app and grab them on your next trip.

- The reference opens with a question hook, speaks to "your favorite sodas", and ends with
  an action ("check your app"); the model's line is a flat, inert listing. Both orders
  agreed. This is exactly what the 0.6-weighted judge term exists for: the gate certifies
  truthfulness, the judge scores quality — and quality is where this rollout paid.

## Results and per-instance variance

Protocol: eval_unseen, 50 instances × 8 attempts per level at temperature 1.0; both models
judged with 0 errors. `final` in the table is the mean over gate-passed attempts (as in the
report); `zero-var` / `within-sd` are computed over **all 8 attempts with gate failures
counted as 0** (the on-policy view; not carried in the report — recomputed 2026-10-25 from
rollouts + judged sidecars).

| model | level | gate pass | judge win | final (passers) [95% CI] | zero-var | within-sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L1 | 88% | 0.51 | 0.62 [0.59, 0.65] | 2% | 0.225 |
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L2 | 86% | 0.54 | 0.64 [0.61, 0.67] | 4% | 0.239 |
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L3 | 77% | 0.63 | 0.70 [0.67, 0.73] | 10% | 0.261 |
| qwen/qwen3.8-27b | L1 | 72% | 0.52 | 0.63 [0.60, 0.66] | 6% | 0.263 |
| qwen/qwen3.8-27b | L2 | 73% | 0.53 | 0.64 [0.60, 0.67] | 12% | 0.243 |
| qwen/qwen3.8-27b | L3 | 64% | 0.56 | 0.65 [0.62, 0.69] | 16% | 0.259 |

- Among gate-passed attempts the two models are statistically indistinguishable at L1/L2
  (0.62/0.64 vs 0.63/0.64, CIs overlapping; judge wins equal at 0.51–0.54 vs 0.52–0.53). At
  L3 luna pulls ~5pp ahead on final (0.70 vs 0.65), driven by the judge term (win 0.63 vs
  0.56). The dominant gap between the models is the **gate**: 88/86/77% vs 72/73/64%, with
  the same dominant mode (`ungrounded_category`) — a single learnable rule, ideal RL shape.
- luna's judge win *rises* with level (0.51 → 0.63): with 19 distractors, template-like
  references are easier to beat, while grounded picking gets harder. qwen writes ~20 words
  vs luna's ~12–13 and passes the gate less often.
- Reward varies substantially across attempts on the same instance (within-sd ≈ 0.22–0.26;
  only 2–16% of instances are zero-variance) — every instance is a coin-flip between gate
  pass and fail for qwen, and between judge win and loss for both. High-variance,
  non-saturated reward is exactly the regime on-policy RL wants.

# Cart optimization (cartopt)

Task card. Numbers from the eval_unseen sweeps of 2026-10-24/25; the live version of every
table is regenerated by `scripts/cartopt/analyze.py` into `reports/cartopt/eval_report.md`.

## The problem it solves

A shopper has a list of categories to buy, one item each. The menu carries real weekly shelf
prices and loyalty discounts, coupons stack (item coupons, spend-threshold bundles,
manufacturer multi-buys), and there may be side constraints (brand quotas, required items).
The cheapest *valid* cart is usually not the cart of per-category cheapest items — coupons
and constraints interact.

**Potential use cases:** agentic shopping assistants and smart cart completion ("finish my
list as cheaply as possible"), coupon-aware substitution recommendations in grocery
e-commerce, and a compact, exactly-gradable stand-in for constrained purchasing decisions
in RL.

Instances are built on the dunnhumby Complete Journey dataset: real products, real weekly
prices, anchored on real household baskets on campaign-live days. SKU codes are neutral
shuffled identifiers — they leak no category or menu position. The model sees the shopping
list, menu table, coupons and constraints, and must end its reply with exactly one block:

```text
<answer>{"cart": {"<category>": "<code>", ...}, "total": <dollars>}</answer>
```

## Reward function

Weights (`configs/cartopt/reward.yaml`): `w_partial` 0.3, `w_feasible` 0.5, `w_opt` 0.4,
`w_price` 0.1; tie epsilon 1c. Reward ∈ [0, 1].

| outcome | reward |
|---|---|
| parse failure (no/multiple `<answer>` block, invalid JSON, non-numeric total) | 0.0 |
| infeasible cart | 0.3 × (share of constraint checks passed) — at most 0.3 |
| feasible cart | 0.5 + 0.4·opt + 0.1·price_ok — from 0.5 to 1.0 |

- `opt = clip((C_ref − cost) / (C_ref − C*), 0, 1)` — linear credit between the median
  feasible cart cost `C_ref` (opt 0) and the exact optimum `C*` (opt 1, within 1c).
- `exact_opt`: recomputed cost ≤ C* + 1c; this is the pass@k success key. Instances whose
  runner-up cart is within 10c of the optimum are rejected at generation, so "exact" is
  unambiguous.
- `price_ok`: the claimed `total` is within 1c of the cost the grader recomputes from the
  cart. Claiming a lower total does not change `cost`, `opt` or feasibility — only this
  0.1 term.
- `C*` and `C_ref` come from an exact solver, never from the model.

Levels scale the combinatorics (`configs/cartopt/levels.yaml`):

| level | categories × SKUs | SKU coupons | threshold bundles | brand quota | cart space |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 4 × 3 | 1–2 | 0 | – | 81 |
| L2 | 5 × 4 | 2–3 | 1 | yes | 1,024 |
| L3 | 6 × 4 | 2–4 | 2 | yes | 4,096 |
| L4 | 8 × 5 | 3–5 | 3 | yes | 390,625 |

**Trap stratum.** 70% of instances are traps: the greedy per-category-cheapest cart is
infeasible or more than 1c above C*. Solving them requires reading coupons and constraints,
not price-sorting. On half the L1 instances a "gap cap" additionally shrinks one coupon so
the couponed SKU stays 15–65c above the cheapest alternative — defeating an "always take
the couponed SKU" shortcut.

## Why it won't be reward hacked

The reward is computed from the cart and the pricing engine, not from anything the model
asserts:

- **Claimed totals are inert.** The grader recomputes cost from the chosen SKUs
  (`src/commgym/cartopt/pricing.py`); the `total` field only feeds the 0.1 `price_ok` term.
  Understating it cannot raise optimality (`tests/cartopt/test_reward_hacking.py::
  test_oracle_weights_and_total_penalties`).
- **The optimum is independent of the model.** C* comes from exhaustive enumeration of the
  whole cart space (hard cap 1M carts), cross-checked against a second, independently
  written implementation on 20 random instances — costs, feasibility and C* match exactly
  (`tests/cartopt/test_solver.py`).
- **Structural cheats are enumerated violations.** Dropping a category (`missing:<cat>`),
  inventing codes (`bad_code:`), a valid code under the wrong category (`wrong_category:`),
  unlisted categories (`extra_categories`), breaking quotas (`quota:National/Private`) or
  ignoring a required SKU (`loyal:`) each cost feasibility — and infeasible reward (≤ 0.3)
  is structurally below the feasible floor (0.5). Property tests pin that *any* infeasible
  cart scores below *any* feasible cart, over arbitrary generated text.
- **A cheaper-but-invalid cart wins nothing.** A cart costing less than C* must be
  infeasible and lands in the ≤ 0.3 tier (`test_greedy_trap_scores_below_oracle`) — see
  failure example 1 below for a live case.
- **Parse gaming dies at 0.** Exactly one `<answer>` block with valid JSON and a numeric
  total; anything else scores 0.0, including two answer blocks or JSON outside the tags
  (hypothesis-tested in `tests/cartopt/test_properties.py`).
- **Partial credit is honest.** `opt` is 0 at the median feasible cost, so a random
  feasible cart earns nothing toward the 0.4 term
  (`test_random_feasible_opts_center_at_zero`).
- **Shortcut policies are measured and lose.** `always_private` and `always_coupon_sku`
  baselines are graded against the oracle in `tests/cartopt/test_baselines.py`.
- **Traps are the point.** With 70% traps and near-ties filtered, the only reliable route
  to reward 1.0 is finding the true optimum.

## Instances

| split | instances | note |
|---|---|---|
| dev | 30 per level × L1–L4 = 120 | cohort B households, weeks 32–68 |
| eval_unseen | 100 per level × L1–L4 = 400 | cohort B households, weeks 69+ (unseen households, later window) |
| train | 0 generated so far | generator supports `--split train` (cohort A, weeks 32–68); refund is currently the only task with train instances |

20% of eligible commodities are held out (seeded) and never sampled into any split — an
unseen-commodity generalization axis.

### Success 3 — L2 with visible reasoning, `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`

Instance `eval_unseen-L2-83`, rollout 0/8, stratum trap. C* = $9.98, median feasible
$14.82. Quota: at least 4 National items. The model's reasoning (excerpt):

> Step 2: Check the National constraint — DELI MEATS and LUNCHMEAT are all National, giving
> 2. I need 2 more from MISC. DAIRY, CHEESE, EGGS. The cheapest way to get 2 more National
> items: MISC. DAIRY → B6 (National, $1.89) — only $0.70 more than cheapest (B7); EGGS →
> H8 (National, $2.29) — only $0.96 more than cheapest (N9). This leaves CHEESE as Private.
> Step 3: With V8 in cart → K2 applies: −$0.55. Checking K3: EGGS + LUNCHMEAT = $4.28 <
> $6.04 → doesn't apply. Step 4: $10.53 − $0.55 = $9.98.

```json
<answer>{"cart": {"DELI MEATS": "Z5", "LUNCHMEAT": "L8", "MISC. DAIRY": "B6", "CHEESE": "V8", "EGGS": "H8"}, "total": 9.98}</answer>
```

**Grade: reward 1.0** — gap 0c, price_ok true. The cheapest cart (B7/N9 picks) would break
the National quota; the model priced the quota exactly ($0.70 + $0.96) and took the coupon.

### Failure 1 — cheaper but infeasible (quota), `openai/gpt-6-luna`

Instance `eval_unseen-L2-14`, rollout 0/8, stratum trap. C* = $7.73. Coupons included a
$1.50 bundle on DRY MIX DESSERTS + CHEESE ≥ $2.25 and a $0.75 item coupon on V4; constraint:
at least 4 National-brand items.

```json
<answer>{"cart":{"CHEESE":"N3","COLD CEREAL":"C3","FD WRAPS/BAGS/TRSH BG":"H0","DRY MIX DESSERTS":"C1","COOKIES/CONES":"V4"},"total":6.98}</answer>
```

**Grade: reward 0.257** — violations `['quota:National']`, 6 of 7 checks passed (0.3 × 6/7).
The cart costs $6.98, i.e. 75c *below* the optimum — but it holds only three National items.
A cart cheaper than C* is necessarily invalid, and the 0.3 infeasible cap guarantees it is
worth less than any feasible cart (floor 0.5).

### Failure 2 — right cart, wrong arithmetic, `openai/gpt-6-luna`

Instance `eval_unseen-L4-14`, rollout 2/8, stratum trap. C* = $7.38.

```json
<answer>{"cart":{"ROLLS":"V2","FROZEN":"M8","CHEESE":"J6","DELI MEATS":"G1","WATER - CARBONATED/FLVRD DRINK":"R8","CONDIMENTS/SAUCES":"N5","APPLES":"W0","LUNCHMEAT":"N2"},"total":5.88}</answer>
```

**Grade: reward 0.9** — the cart is the exact optimum (gap 0c, feasible), but the claimed
total $5.88 is $1.50 below the true cost $7.38, so `price_ok` fails. The decomposition
0.5 + 0.4·1.0 + 0.1·0 shows exactly what was lost: the arithmetic term, nothing else.

### Failure 3 — the threshold-bundle trap, `openai/gpt-6-luna`

Instance `eval_unseen-L2-302`, rollout 2/8. C* = $10.72, median feasible $42.70. The
CIGARETTES menu rows and the coupons:

```text
| K9 | CIGARETTES | National | 539 | 515676 CTN | $22.19 | $0.00 |
| N0 | CIGARETTES | National | 509 | CTN | $3.74 | $0.00 |

- K2: $1.00 off item B1
- K3: $0.75 off when the combined net spend on HEAT/SERVE, CIGARETTES reaches $23.80
```

```json
<answer>{"cart":{"APPLES":"N5","HEAT/SERVE":"B1","CIGARETTES":"K9","LAUNDRY DETERGENTS":"S3","CANDY - PACKAGED":"Y5"},"total":28.42}</answer>
```

**Grade: reward 0.779** — feasible, price_ok true, but gap 1770c (opt 0.447:
0.5 + 0.4·0.447 + 0.1). The model picked the $22.19 carton to push HEAT/SERVE + CIGARETTES
net spend over the $23.80 threshold and collect $0.75:

| | model cart | solver's optimum |
|---|---|---|
| CIGARETTES | K9 — $22.19 | N0 — $3.74 |
| coupons applied | K2 + K3 = $1.75 | K2 = $1.00 |
| total | $28.42 | $10.72 |

It spent $18.45 more on cigarettes to earn $0.75 back. This is the failure mode the trap
stratum is built to produce, and the graded lesson a shopper-agent needs: a coupon that
requires spending past the cheapest option is usually a loss.

## Results and per-instance variance

Protocol: eval_unseen, 8 attempts per instance at temperature 1.0 (max_tokens 8192,
reasoning low). luna ran 100 instances per level; qwen ran a 10-instance-per-level subset at
L1–L2 only — at L3/L4 it exhausts the 8192-token budget before emitting the answer block
(`parse:no_answer_block`), which is a token-budget artifact, not a capability result, so
those rollouts were discarded rather than reported.

`zero-var` = share of instances where all 8 attempts earned the identical reward (no
learning gradient for on-policy RL); `within-sd` = mean per-instance SD of reward across the
8 attempts.

| model | level | inst × k | mean reward [95% CI] | pass@1 (exact opt) | zero-var | within-sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L1 | 100 × 8 | 0.996 [0.994, 0.998] | 97% | 77% | 0.008 |
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L2 | 100 × 8 | 0.943 [0.929, 0.955] | 69% | 7% | 0.093 |
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L3 | 100 × 8 | 0.945 [0.934, 0.955] | 64% | 7% | 0.089 |
| openai/gpt-6-luna | L4 | 100 × 8 | 0.915 [0.898, 0.930] | 47% | 3% | 0.123 |
| qwen/qwen3.8-27b | L1 | 10 × 8 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 100% | 100% | 0.000 |
| qwen/qwen3.8-27b | L2 | 10 × 8 | 0.852 [0.718, 0.959] | 71% | 50% | 0.144 |

- At L1 the task is saturated for both models (luna 97% pass@1, qwen 100% on its subset);
  77%/100% zero-variance means no gradient — L1 is a sanity tier, not a training tier.
- L2–L4 carry the signal: luna's mean stays high but pass@1 drops 69 → 64 → 47%, and the
  failure mix shifts from suboptimal-feasible to arithmetic and quota violations. At L4 the
  trap stratum alone is 0.891 mean / 38% pass@1 vs 0.970 / 68% on no-trap instances.
- Between attempts on the same instance, reward varies meaningfully from L2 up (within-sd
  0.09–0.12 for luna) — the same instance is solved on some attempts and missed on others,
  which is exactly the shape on-policy methods exploit.
